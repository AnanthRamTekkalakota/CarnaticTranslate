The short version

CarnaticTranslate does not require a separate CarnaticTranslate account and does not include third-party advertising, analytics, or crash-reporting SDKs. A scan sends its source image and transcription instructions through a Cloudflare Worker we operate to a configured AI provider, with separate handling for a configured custom endpoint as described below. Saved documents and source images remain on your device or, if you opt in, in your iCloud account. The app and Worker also keep the operational, recovery, purchase, and security records described below.

Source images and AI transcription

For the app-supplied transcription service, each scanned page is sent to our Cloudflare Worker with the transcription prompt and any continuation context needed for a multi-page scan. The Worker validates the request, applies security and page-allowance checks, forwards the image and instructions to the AI provider determined by the Worker service configuration, validates the response, and returns a normalized transcription to the app. Provider handling may differ by provider, account, and configuration. The applicable provider terms and privacy practices govern that provider's processing in addition to this policy.

The Worker buffers the request and provider response while processing the scan. Its application code does not deliberately write the source image, prompt, or raw provider response to Worker logs or ordinary Durable Object records. A successfully validated transcription is handled separately for short-term result retrieval as described below. Cloudflare still processes the request as the infrastructure provider; application-code behavior alone does not establish every account-level, edge, support-access, or internal-retention setting at Cloudflare.

In builds that expose a custom OpenAI-compatible endpoint, if you configure one, the app sends the image, instructions, and your endpoint credential directly to that endpoint instead of using our Worker. The custom operator's terms and privacy practices apply, and we do not control them. OpenAI-compatible describes an API format; it does not establish that OpenAI operates the endpoint.

AI providers and routing

Intended release: Anthropic and OpenAI are the intended AI processors for bundled transcription through Cloudflare. The service determines routing; this is not a promise that a scan goes to both providers, that you can choose a provider, or that providers differ by plan. Production routing for this intended release has not yet been confirmed.

Established implementation: the locally prepared automatic route selects Anthropic and permits at most one retry of the same model, with no automatic fallback to OpenAI or Google. Explicit OpenAI routes exist in the Worker but are disabled by its checked-in explicit-selection setting. An OpenAI code path alone does not establish that OpenAI is currently enabled in production.

Earlier builds and other paths: earlier configurations supported Google’s Gemini API, and legacy Gemini routes remain in the source. Google is excluded from the intended release. Content submitted through an earlier Google route was subject to Google’s Gemini API terms and data-use provisions; a change in release providers does not erase earlier provider-held copies. A custom endpoint, where exposed, is a separate direct route governed by its operator’s practices.

Short-term Worker result retrieval

To recover a successful scan when a response is lost, the Worker stores the normalized transcription response and served-model value as application-encrypted ciphertext. A matching request from the same pseudonymous owner, operation, and input may retrieve that result for up to 15 minutes without another provider call or page charge. The Worker does not place the source image or prompt in this recovery ciphertext.

After the retrieval window expires, the ciphertext and encryption IV become eligible for active deletion by a Worker alarm or a later cleanup or admission operation. Eligibility and active deletion are separate: Cloudflare does not guarantee that an alarm runs at the exact expiry time. The operation UUID, request-body hash, allowance link, operation state, expiry, and deletion time remain in active service storage without an application-level expiry. In addition, Cloudflare's SQLite point-in-time recovery can restore prior Durable Object state from a point within the previous 30 days (Cloudflare’s SQLite/PITR documentation). Active deletion therefore does not erase copies that may remain in that point-in-time recovery history.

Operational records and identifiers

The app and Worker use pseudonymous identifiers to secure the service, enforce page allowances, and make retries idempotent. These include a random device UUID stored in Keychain, an Apple App Attest key and receipt, a per-page operation UUID, a random Worker request ID, server-created owner and account identifiers, and HMAC-derived Apple transaction lookup keys. Cloudflare also processes ordinary network metadata, such as IP addresses, when delivering requests. The Worker uses the raw device UUID and IP address transiently to derive throttle selectors; our custom logs do not include the raw device UUID or IP address.

Worker storage contains App Attest registration and counter state, opaque owner bindings, subscription grants and dates, page usage and reservations, throttle counts, and operation records. Raw Apple transaction identifiers, signed Apple proofs, notification bodies, source images, prompts, and transcription text are not deliberately stored in these records. Except for the specific expiring records described in this policy, these pseudonymous service records currently have no application-level deletion schedule or user deletion endpoint.

The Worker emits custom operational logs for each request. Depending on the route, these logs may include the random request ID, route and outcome, requested and served provider/model, provider status, latency and duration, fallback or retry result, token counts, finish reason, App Attest outcome and timing, plan and allowance counts and dates, and the page operation UUID. They do not deliberately include images, prompts, transcription bodies, provider credentials, raw Apple proofs, or raw Apple transaction identifiers. Worker observability is configured for full head sampling, while Cloudflare automatic invocation logs are disabled in the checked-in configuration. Cloudflare account settings and plan determine how long Workers Logs remain available and whether any account-level export exists; no log export is configured in the application source.

AI-provider use and retention

Provider handling is separate from our Worker storage. The following are published API/commercial defaults, not confirmation of the arrangements or controls on the accounts used by CarnaticTranslate.

  • Anthropic: its commercial guidance says submitted content is not used for model training by default; explicit sharing or feedback may permit training. Its standard API retention policy provides deletion of inputs and outputs within 30 days, with exceptions for agreed terms, features with longer retention, safety enforcement and law. Covered models can have separate retention and review requirements. See commercial training practices, API retention and exceptions, API retention controls and Commercial Terms.
  • OpenAI: its API documentation says API data is not used to train models unless the customer opts in. Default abuse-monitoring logs can include prompts and responses and are retained for up to 30 days, with longer retention possible for legal or safety reasons. Some API features separately retain application state; endpoint, feature and storage settings matter. See API data controls and endpoint retention and the Services Agreement.

Account-specific contracts, workspace settings, voluntary data sharing, retention exceptions and access arrangements for the intended release have not been confirmed. We do not promise zero data retention, account-specific training settings, or different privacy treatment for Free and paid plans. Consumer chatbot settings do not establish API data handling. A custom operator may have different practices even when its endpoint accepts OpenAI-compatible requests.

Saved documents and source images

Saved documents contain the transcription, metadata you enter (such as title, ragam, talam, and composer), and references to downscaled source-page images. The document JSON and source images are stored in the app's document storage. Generated PDFs are derived files kept in a local cache. The Worker does not become the primary storage location for your saved library.

Optional iCloud sync

iCloud sync is optional and off by default. If you enable it, document JSON and associated source-page images move to the app's iCloud ubiquity container and can appear on your other devices using the same iCloud account. Generated PDFs remain in the local cache. Apple handles iCloud data under its own iCloud terms and privacy policy. Turning sync off leaves existing iCloud copies in place. Saved document JSON and source images may also be included in device or computer backups; the exclusions below apply to managed working and recovery files, not the primary saved library.

Local migration backups

When the app successfully copies and verifies a local document or image during migration to iCloud, it moves the local original to a migration-backup folder and records its provenance. A verified, recorded migration backup becomes eligible for cleanup seven days after verification. Failed or unverified migration originals do not become cleanup candidates. Older or legacy files whose provenance is unknown also remain protected because the app does not infer ownership from age or filename alone. The managed migration-backup folder is excluded from device backup, but that exclusion cannot remove copies already present in historical backups or snapshots.

Unfinished scans and temporary exports

For background scanning and recovery after interruption, the app stores prepared source-image files, exact serialized request bodies, page operation IDs, settings, completed page results, status and error information, retry counts, and timestamps on the device. These working files are excluded from device backup. A completed job is removed only after its result is successfully saved as a document or you explicitly discard it. Failed, interrupted, and completed-but-unsaved work remains available for recovery or review until you save or discard it.

Exports are created as temporary local files. A file being actively shared is protected from cleanup. When sharing finishes, the app attempts immediate removal. If removal fails, or if a prior-process share can no longer be treated as active, the file is marked abandoned and becomes eligible for cleanup after 24 hours.

iCloud conflict recovery

When iCloud reports conflicting document versions, the app selects the newest JSON version and first archives each losing JSON version in device-local Application Support. These archives are excluded from device backup and do not sync across devices. They preserve document JSON only; referenced source images are not copied into the archive. Recovery creates a new document without those source images. An archive becomes eligible for cleanup after 30 days, unless it is actively being recovered.

Deletion and remaining copies

When iCloud sync is active, deleting a document removes its active JSON, referenced source images, and cached PDF from the active stores and requests deletion from iCloud so the change can propagate to devices with sync enabled. When sync is off, unavailable, or unresolved and the app is using local storage, deletion removes only the local active copy. A copy left in iCloud may reappear if you enable sync again.

Deleting a document in the app concerns its active local and, when sync is enabled, iCloud copies; it does not delete Worker account, allowance, security, or operation records. The service does not currently provide an authenticated in-app workflow to request deletion of developer-held server data. You may contact Ananth Ram Tekkalakota at razer323@hotmail.com with questions or to request help concerning your information, including access, correction or deletion requests where applicable. Do not email receipts, signed Apple proofs, API keys, passwords or device identifiers. We will explain any ownership-verification requirements and the scope and limitations of the response. Sending an email is not an automated deletion command and does not by itself delete data. No immediate or complete erasure is promised.

Apple subscription cancellation is separate from both document deletion and a privacy request. Manage or cancel an App Store subscription in Apple’s subscription settings; deleting documents, contacting us, or removing the app does not itself cancel it. Active deletion by us or Apple does not necessarily remove historical platform backups or provider retention copies. Those copies may remain under the applicable platform, provider, or account retention rules, which have not all been verified for the launch configuration.

Before removing managed conflict archives for a deleted document, the app records a local deletion tombstone. If physical archive removal fails, the archive is hidden from recovery and cleanup is retried so that managed archive cannot recreate the deleted document. Explicit document deletion also attempts to remove migration backups whose ownership is established by recorded metadata. Unindexed legacy archives, unknown-provenance migration backups, copies already present in device or iCloud backups or snapshots, and other copies outside the active store may remain. Deleting active data does not erase those historical backups, and their retention and purge timing are controlled by the operating system or backup provider.

When local cleanup runs

Seven-day, 24-hour, and 30-day periods in this policy describe when a local item becomes eligible for cleanup, not a guaranteed deletion time. Cleanup runs at safe app-execution opportunities, including launch and return to the foreground. The app cannot promise cleanup while it is closed, and failed deletions retain lifecycle metadata so they can be retried later.

Credentials

Custom-provider and bring-your-own-key credentials, the active background-job credential, and the stable device UUID are stored in Apple Keychain with after-first-unlock accessibility so background recovery can operate after the device has been unlocked once. The app migrates the former plaintext custom-provider key from preferences to Keychain and removes the preference only after the Keychain copy is verified. If migration fails, the legacy value is retained so it is not lost. Removing a live plaintext preference cannot erase copies that may already exist in historical device or iCloud backups or snapshots.

On-device diagnostics and notifications

The app emits local console and performance-signpost diagnostics used to operate and debug app features. Depending on the build and action, these can include the backend environment and URL, requested or served model, HTTP status, duration, random Worker request ID, error category or message, App Attest outcome, StoreKit product IDs and coarse verified-state evidence, scan page and task counts, parsing structure, export format and document structure counts, byte counts, and an exported file's name and local path. The app does not send these diagnostics to a third-party analytics or crash-reporting SDK. Device and development tools may retain or expose local logs under their own settings. If you enable notifications or Live Activities, the app may display a transcribed song title on the lock screen when reporting scan completion.

No sale of information or advertising analytics

The app does not ask for your name, email address, contacts, precise location, or advertising identifier, and we do not sell your information. The app does not include third-party advertising, analytics, or crash-reporting SDKs. This statement does not exclude the operational Worker logs, Apple service records, provider processing, or local diagnostic and notification surfaces described in this policy. If you contact support, your email address and the information you choose to send are processed to respond to your request; do not include sensitive scan content or credentials.

Age eligibility and children’s privacy

The intended service minimum is 13 years old. The service is not directed to children under 13. If you are under 18, you must have your parent or legal guardian’s permission to use the service and submit content for AI processing. Local age, privacy and consent requirements also apply. Where local law requires parental authorization for consent-based processing, that authorization is required before submission. See age eligibility in the Terms.

This draft does not represent that age verification or a parental-consent mechanism is implemented. Apple’s content age rating is separate from service eligibility. If you believe a child under 13 has submitted personal information, contact us using the address below so we can assess the request and explain the available response and its limits.

Accuracy notice

Transcriptions are generated by AI and may contain errors. Always review and verify output against the original source before relying on it.
